# Validation Corpus — Tranche 02 v0.1

**Status: ACTIVE / PARTIAL**  
**Cases: VC-0021..VC-0060**  
**Milestone: Research Foundation v0.1**

This tranche expands the benchmark from 20 to **60 evidence-backed or evidence-seeded hard cases**. It is designed to stress multilingual Indian production identity, title/release history, streaming partitioning, anthology structure, anime/adaptation lineage, alternate cuts, platform migration, episode ordering and organization identity.

Evidence labels follow the corpus convention:
- **A** first-party/official/legal source directly competent for the claim.
- **B** institutional/archive/specialist source.
- **C** reputable secondary/reference source; requires stronger corroboration before final gold freeze where identity risk is high.

A case may be useful before it reaches gold status. The anti-gaming rule still applies: failing cases are fixed, not deleted.

# Findings from Tranche 02

## F-06 — Work and language realization need an explicit multilingual-production rule
Baahubali and Saaho show that simultaneous filming in multiple languages cannot be modeled as `one original + all others dubs`, nor as separate remakes. V1 needs explicit `production_language_realization` / Version semantics.

## F-07 — Season identity and release partition are different concepts
Stranger Things, The Crown, Cobra Kai, Bridgerton, Ozark, Manifest and Money Heist demonstrate that `Part`, `Volume`, `Batch` and provider `Part N` labels cannot be hard-coded as Seasons.

## F-08 — Anthology components need first-class containment
Lust Stories, Ajeeb Daastaans and Navarasa require component-level titles/credits/relationships while retaining a parent film/series release identity.

## F-09 — Episode order needs named ordering schemes
The Clone Wars proves that `episode_number` cannot be the sole order. Stable Episode identity must coexist with air, chronological, production and provider orders when supported.

## F-10 — Corporate history cannot mutate historical credits
WBD, MGM/Amazon and JioStar cases require temporal Organization relationships (`acquired_by`, `merged_into`, `formed_from`, `renamed_to`, `owned_by`) rather than destructive renaming.

## F-11 — platform identity is independent of Series/Work identity
Manifest and other platform migrations show that a title does not become a new Series because its network/streamer changes.

## F-12 — title history needs role + locale + effective time
Working titles (`Project K`, `Thalapathy 69`, `VD12`), market titles (`Saamraajya`) and final title changes (`Laxmii`) are semantically different and need explicit title-kind and effective-time fields.

## F-13 — alternate-cut boundary must support an adjudication escape hatch
Justice League/Zack Snyder's Justice League demonstrates that some cases are not well served by a rigid universal `always Version` or `always Work` rule. Schema must support an auditable relationship decision without losing source facts.
